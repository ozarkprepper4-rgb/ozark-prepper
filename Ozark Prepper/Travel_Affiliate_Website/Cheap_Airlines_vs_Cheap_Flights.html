Cheap Airlines vs Cheap Flights: How to Pick the Right One

Written in plain words. You do not need much money to fly. The words “airline” and “airline ticket site” mean different things. This page shows the difference so you pick what fits you.

What is a “cheap airline”?

A cheap airline is a company that sells you a seat on a plane. Some charge for everything on top. Some give you more for your money.

Examples in this guide: - Southwest — no carry-on fee, no seat fee. - Frontier — lowest prices, but carries bags cost money. - Allegiant — cheap, but no free changes or free carry-on.

What is a “cheap flight”?

A cheap flight is the ticket price you pay to fly from one place to another. You can book a ticket with a travel site (Google Flights, Expedia) or a direct airline site.

Some travel sites let you compare many airlines fast. That is a tool, not a cheaper airline.

How to pick

  1. Pick your airports. Cheap seats are often at smaller airports.
  2. Fly mid-week. Tuesday and Wednesday are often cheaper than Friday or Sunday.
  3. Choose early morning or late night. These seats are often cheaper.
  4. Set a price alert. Many sites let you do this free.
  5. Check carry-on. Cheap airlines often charge for bags.

My honest pick for you

If you want the least stress and one bag is free: Southwest. If you want the lowest price and you travel with only a personal item: Frontier or Allegiant, and pack light.
