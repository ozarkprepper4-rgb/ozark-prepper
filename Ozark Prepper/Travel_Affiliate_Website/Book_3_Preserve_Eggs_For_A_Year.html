What this book is about

If you keep chickens you can keep eggs for a year with a few simple, tested methods. This book covers the two safest ways: water-glassing and plain refrigeration. You learn which works, what the risks are, and exactly how to do it.

Chapter 1: Know your eggs first

Fresh, clean eggs from your own hens are the best to save. Eggs that are dirty, cracked, or from a sick bird should not be preserved. Wash only what you will use right away. Washing removes the shell’s natural guard.

Chapter 2: The water-glass method (for eggs you will not use for months)

You need food-grade lime and water. The classic mix is one part pickling lime to one part water, then a tablespoon or two of plain white vinegar to make the water cloudy and the whole thing ready. 1. Put the pickling-lime mixture into a clean jar and stir until the water turns cloudy. 2. Set the clean, dry eggs into the jar so they are fully covered. 3. Seal the jar and keep it in a cool, dark place. 4. Leave the eggs for several months and check them every few weeks.

Chapter 3: The plain-keep (refrigerator) method

For the eggs you will use soon, put them in the fridge in their original cartons or a covered container. Keep them away from strong-smelling foods. Use within a few weeks.

Chapter 4: How to tell if a preserved egg is still good

Never eat a preserved egg that smells bad or looks odd. Crack it into a bowl and smell it first. If it floats in water, throw it out. A fresh egg sinks; a bad one stands up or floats.

Safety warning

Never consume a preserved egg that smells bad, looks off, or has an unusual colour. When in doubt, throw it out. This book is a guide, not a promise of safety. Test with a small batch first.
